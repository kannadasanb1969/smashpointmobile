# SmashPoint Business Rules

This document records behavior evidenced by the current mobile implementation and its API contracts. Backend validation remains authoritative for persisted business decisions.

## Identity Model

- A User Account has a server-issued role and authentication session.
- A workspace is the active `PLAYER`, `ORGANIZER`, or `ADMIN` context.
- A Player Profile represents the participant identity used by registrations, Friendly Matches, teams, fixtures, and results.
- Tournament ownership and player participation are separate concepts. An organizer may participate through a Player Profile when normal registration rules allow it.

## Roles

- `PLAYER` — manages a player profile, joins events, participates in Friendly Matches, and views fixtures, scores, results, medals, and notifications.
- `ORGANIZER` — creates and manages tournaments, registrations, fixtures, matches, and organizer views.
- `ADMIN` — performs administrative review and management workflows.

The backend issues role context; the client does not grant itself permissions.

## Tournament

The mobile app supports tournament creation, category configuration, approval/review workflows, registration, fixture generation, live matches, results, and medals. Organizer ownership controls management operations; participation uses the normal player registration model.

## Tournament Registration

Registration screens support eligibility responses from the backend, duplicate prevention, Singles and Doubles events, partner selection, and guest-player context where supplied by the API. The exact eligibility decision is server-owned.

## Friendly Match

Friendly Matches have a creator who retains ownership and management authority. The creator is a Player, not globally promoted to Organizer. The creator can use the explicit “Join as Player” behavior where supported. Other players use the request → approve/reject flow. Approval creates the participant; rejection does not.

Supported formats are `LEAGUE` and `KNOCKOUT`, with `SINGLES` and `DOUBLES` event types.

## Friendly Singles

The current client validates a minimum of 6 players and a maximum of 16 players for Singles Friendly Matches. Participants are persisted as player identities.

## Friendly Doubles

Doubles require at least 8 players, an even player count, and no more than 16 players. Approved players can be manually paired, remaining players can be shuffled, and teams preserve their team identity through fixture and match flows.

## League

League fixtures create round-robin match sets and standings. League flows are separate from knockout progression. Pool-aware tournament behavior is represented in the app’s fixture and standings views where returned by the API.

## Knockout and BYEs

Knockout fixtures display rounds and bracket progression. A BYE or an upstream winner can populate the appropriate next-round slot. The backend owns progression and source/next-match relationships; the client displays the resulting state and does not invent winners.

## Scoring

The organizer and Friendly Match scoring UI supports winning targets of 15, 21, or 30. The target is not a hard ceiling: when scores are tied at or beyond the target, play can continue. Completion requires reaching the configured target and having the required two-point lead. For example, at target 21, 21–20 is not complete, 22–21 is not complete, and 23–21 may complete.

## Fixtures

Fixtures are generated by the backend and displayed by role-specific screens. The app supports rounds, pools/brackets where provided, participant/team identities, match status, and navigation to match centre screens.

## Results, Medals, and Notifications

The app displays completed Friendly Match and tournament results, winner/runner-up information, medal context, and notification data returned by the backend. It does not independently finalize authoritative results.

## Protected Business Behavior

Authentication, registration, ownership, creator self-join, approvals, Singles/Doubles pairing, League, Knockout, BYE progression, fixtures, live scoring, results, medals, notifications, and session behavior must not change as a side effect of UI cleanup or architectural refactoring. Any intentional change requires explicit scope and regression testing.
